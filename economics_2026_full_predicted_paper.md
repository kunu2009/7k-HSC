## Q.2: Identify and Explain Concepts (6 Marks)
### *(Attempt any THREE out of FIVE — 2 Marks each)*

Based on PYQs 2022-2025, Q.2 presents a real-life situation/statement and asks you to identify the underlying economic concept. These are the **5 most predicted scenarios**:

---

**Q.2.1)** *"Gauri collected information about the income earned by a particular firm in the city."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **Concept: Micro Economics**
>
> Micro Economics is the study of individual economic units — individual consumer, individual firm, individual price. Gauri is studying the income of a "particular firm" which is an individual unit. It is also called **Price Theory**. Father: **Alfred Marshall** (1890). It uses the **Slicing method** and **Partial equilibrium** analysis.

---

**Q.2.2)** *"Bhushan refused to eat the 5th chapati after eating 4 chapatis."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **Concept: Point of Satiety (Law of DMU)**
>
> The Point of Satiety is where the Marginal Utility (MU) of a commodity becomes **zero** and Total Utility (TU) is at its **maximum**. After consuming 4 chapatis, Bhushan's MU = 0. Eating the 5th would give **negative utility** (disutility). A rational consumer stops at the point of satiety.

---

**Q.2.3)** *"The cost incurred by the firm to promote the sale of its product through advertisements, free gifts, and window displays."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **Concept: Selling Cost**
>
> Selling cost is the expenditure incurred by the firm to promote sales. It includes advertisements, TV broadcasts, hoardings, free gifts, window displays, exhibitions, etc. It is a **peculiar feature of Monopolistic Competition** where product differentiation exists and firms compete for customers.

---

**Q.2.4)** *"Sheetal purchased wheat flour for her bakery from the flour mill."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **Concept: Intermediate Goods**
>
> Intermediate goods are those used for **further production**, not for final consumption. The wheat flour purchased by a bakery is an intermediate good as it will be used to produce bread/cakes. Its value is **excluded from National Income** to avoid **double counting**.

---

**Q.2.5)** *"India purchased petroleum from Iran."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **Concept: Import Trade**
>
> Import trade refers to the **purchase of goods/services from another country** — inflow of goods from a foreign country to the home country. India buying petroleum from Iran is an example of import trade, which is one of the three types of foreign trade (Import, Export, Entrepot).

## Q.3: Distinguish Between (6 Marks)
### *(Attempt any THREE out of FIVE — 2 Marks each)*

These are the **5 most likely "Distinguish Between" pairs** based on recurring PYQ patterns:

---

**Q.3.1)** *Distinguish between Micro Economics and Macro Economics.*

| Basis | Micro Economics | Macro Economics |
|-------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Meaning** | Study of individual units | Study of economy as whole |
| **Also Called** | Price Theory | Income & Employment Theory |
| **Father** | Alfred Marshall | J.M. Keynes |
| **Method** | Slicing method | Lumping method |
| **Analysis** | Partial equilibrium | General equilibrium |
| **Scope** | Worm's eye view (Tree) | Bird's eye view (Forest) |

---

**Q.3.2)** *Distinguish between Total Utility (TU) and Marginal Utility (MU).*

| Basis | Total Utility (TU) | Marginal Utility (MU) |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Meaning** | Sum of all utilities from all units | Additional utility from one more unit |
| **Formula** | TU = Σ MU | MU = TUₙ − TUₙ₋₁ |
| **Curve Shape** | Inverted U-shape | Downward sloping |
| **At Satiety** | TU is **maximum** | MU is **zero** |
| **1st Unit** | TU = MU (both equal) | Highest MU |
| **Negative MU** | TU starts **falling** | MU is below X-axis |

---

**Q.3.3)** *Distinguish between Expansion of Supply and Increase in Supply.*

| Basis | Expansion of Supply | Increase in Supply |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|
| **Cause** | Rise in **price** of commodity | Change in **other factors** (technology, cost) |
| **Price** | Price **changes** (rises) | Price remains **constant** |
| **Curve** | Movement **along** the same curve | **Shift** of entire curve |
| **Direction** | Upward movement on curve | Rightward shift to S₂ |
| **Other Factors** | Remain constant (Ceteris Paribus) | Change |

---

**Q.3.4)** *Distinguish between Direct Tax and Indirect Tax.*

| Basis | Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
|-------|------------|--------------|
| **Burden** | Cannot be shifted | Can be shifted to others |
| **Impact & Incidence** | On **same** person | On **different** persons |
| **Nature** | Progressive | Regressive |
| **Examples** | Income Tax, Wealth Tax | GST, Excise Duty, Customs |
| **Collection** | Directly from taxpayer | Through intermediaries |
| **Evasion** | More possible | Less possible |

---

**Q.3.5)** *Distinguish between Perfectly Elastic Demand and Perfectly Inelastic Demand.*

| Basis | Perfectly Elastic | Perfectly Inelastic |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Coefficient** | Ed = ∞ (infinity) | Ed = 0 (zero) |
| **Curve** | Horizontal (parallel to X-axis) | Vertical (parallel to Y-axis) |
| **Response** | Infinite change in Qd | No change in Qd |
| **Price Change** | Slightest price rise = zero demand | Any price change = same demand |
| **Example** | Theoretical / Perfect competition | Life-saving drugs, Salt (extreme) |

## Q.4: Agree or Disagree with Reasons (12 Marks)
### *(Attempt any THREE out of FIVE — 4 Marks each)*

These are the **5 most predicted "State whether you agree or disagree" statements**:

---

**Q.4.1)** *"The scope of Micro Economics is unlimited."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **No, I disagree with this statement.**
>
> a) The scope of Micro Economics is **LIMITED**, not unlimited.
>
> b) Micro Economics studies only **individual/small economic units** — an individual consumer, an individual firm, an individual price. It has a narrow scope.
>
> c) Micro Economics is like studying a **single tree**, NOT the forest. It takes the **Worm's eye view** of the economy.
>
> d) It uses the **Slicing method** — divides the economy into small parts and studies each separately, under the assumption of **Ceteris Paribus**.
>
> e) It is **Macro Economics** that has a wider scope as it studies the economy as a whole (Bird's eye view).
>
> f) Therefore, the scope of Micro Economics is **limited, not unlimited**.

---

**Q.4.2)** *"There are no exceptions to the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **No, I disagree with this statement.**
>
> a) There **ARE** exceptions to the Law of DMU.
>
> b) **Hobbies (H):** In hobbies like stamp/coin collection, rare paintings — every additional item gives **more** pleasure. MU increases.
>
> c) **Miser (M):** For a miser, every additional rupee gives **more** satisfaction. MU of money increases with stock.
>
> d) **Addictions (A):** For a drunkard, each additional drink increases intoxication. MU **seems** to increase (actually an illusion).
>
> e) **Power (P):** Lust for power increases with power acquired.
>
> f) **Money:** MU of money never becomes zero.
>
> (Shortcode: **H MAP** — Hobbies, Miser, Addictions, Power)

---

**Q.4.3)** *"Demand curve slopes upward from left to right."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **No, I disagree with this statement.**
>
> a) The **demand curve** slopes **downward** from left to right, NOT upward.
>
> b) According to the Law of Demand: "Other things being equal, higher the price, smaller the quantity demanded and lower the price, larger the quantity demanded."
>
> c) There is an **inverse** (negative) relationship between price and quantity demanded.
>
> d) Reasons for downward slope: **Income Effect** (price falls → real income rises), **Substitution Effect** (cheaper goods preferred), and **Law of DMU** (lower price compensates for lower MU).
>
> e) It is the **supply curve** that slopes **upward** from left to right (direct relationship between price and supply).
>
> f) Therefore, the demand curve slopes **downward**, not upward.

---

**Q.4.4)** *"Index numbers can be constructed without the base year."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **No, I disagree with this statement.**
>
> a) Index numbers **cannot** be constructed without a base year.
>
> b) An index number measures changes in an economic variable **over a period of time** — it requires a reference point for comparison.
>
> c) The **base year** (reference year) should be a **normal year**, free from natural calamities, and not too distant in the past.
>
> d) The base year index is always assumed to be **100**, and the current year value is calculated relative to it.
>
> e) Without a base year, there would be no benchmark for comparison, making the index meaningless.
>
> f) Therefore, index numbers **cannot** be constructed without the base year.

---

**Q.4.5)** *"The GST has replaced almost all indirect taxes in India."*

**Predicted Answer:**
> **Yes, I agree with this statement.**
>
> a) **GST** (Goods and Services Tax) came into effect on **July 1, 2017** (101st Constitutional Amendment Act).
>
> b) It is a **comprehensive indirect tax** levied on supply of goods and services across the country.
>
> c) It replaced **17+ indirect taxes** including Excise Duty, Service Tax, VAT, Central Sales Tax, Entry Tax, Octroi, Entertainment Tax, etc.
>
> d) GST follows the principle of **"One Nation, One Tax"** — creating a unified national market.
>
> e) It is a **destination-based tax** (collected where goods are consumed, not where produced).
>
> f) Therefore, GST has indeed replaced almost all indirect taxes in India.

### **Q.6.1)** *"Explain the Law of Demand with a demand schedule, demand curve (diagram), and its exceptions."*

**Predicted Model Answer:**

#### Introduction
The Law of Demand was propounded by **Prof. Alfred Marshall** in his book *"Principles of Economics"* (1890).

#### Statement
*"Other things being equal, higher the price of a commodity, smaller is the quantity demanded and lower the price, larger is the quantity demanded."*

There is an **inverse (negative) relationship** between price and quantity demanded.
Symbolically: **Dx = f(Px)**

#### Assumptions (Ceteris Paribus)
1. Income of the consumer remains constant
2. Prices of related goods (substitutes/complements) remain constant
3. Consumer's tastes, preferences, and habits remain unchanged
4. No expectation of future price change
5. No new substitutes in the market
6. Size of population remains constant

#### Demand Schedule

| Price (₹) | Quantity Demanded (kg) |
|:---------:|:---------------------:|
| 50 | 10 |
| 40 | 20 |
| 30 | 30 |
| 20 | 40 |
| 10 | 50 |

#### Demand Curve
- **X-axis:** Quantity Demanded → **Y-axis:** Price
- The demand curve **DD slopes downward** from left to right
- This confirms the **inverse relationship** between price and quantity demanded

#### Reasons for Downward Slope
1. **Income Effect:** When price falls, consumer's real income rises, enabling more purchases
2. **Substitution Effect:** When price falls, the commodity becomes cheaper relative to substitutes
3. **New Buyers:** Lower prices attract new consumers
4. **Multiple Uses:** At lower prices, goods can be used for additional purposes
5. **Law of DMU:** Each additional unit gives less utility — consumer buys more only at lower price

#### Exceptions to the Law of Demand (Shortcode: **HI GPS**)

1. **H — Habitual Goods:** Tea, cigarettes are purchased in fixed quantities regardless of price due to habit/addiction.

2. **I — Ignorance:** Consumers sometimes buy more at higher prices due to ignorance about cheaper alternatives.

3. **G — Giffen's Paradox:** Sir Robert Giffen observed that when the price of inferior goods (bread) fell, people bought LESS of it and switched to superior goods (meat). Demand moves in the same direction as price.

4. **P — Prestige Goods (Veblen Goods):** Expensive goods like diamonds, luxury cars are status symbols. Rich people buy MORE at higher prices to display wealth.

5. **S — Speculation:** If consumers expect prices to rise further in the future, they buy MORE even at current high prices (e.g., gold, real estate).

6. **Price Illusion:** Consumers believe that higher-priced goods are of better quality.

#### Conclusion
The Law of Demand is a fundamental law of economics that explains consumer behavior. Despite its exceptions, it holds true for the vast majority of commodities and forms the basis of demand analysis.

### **Q.6.2)** *"Explain the Income Method and Expenditure Method of measuring National Income."*

**Predicted Model Answer:**

#### Introduction
National Income is the total monetary value of all **final goods and services** produced in a country during a **financial year** (1st April to 31st March in India). There are three methods to measure it — Output Method, Income Method, and Expenditure Method. The Income and Expenditure methods are explained below.

---

#### A) INCOME METHOD (Factor Cost Method)

**Concept:** This method estimates National Income from the **distribution side**. It adds all **factor incomes** (income payments received by the owners of factors of production) during the year.

**The four factor incomes are:**
1. **Wages (W)** — reward for Labour
2. **Rent (R)** — reward for Land
3. **Interest (I)** — reward for Capital
4. **Profit (P)** — reward for Entrepreneurship

**Formula:**
> **NI = W + R + i + P + MI + (X − M)**
>
> Where MI = Mixed Income of self-employed, (X−M) = Net income from abroad

**Steps:**
1. Identify all producing sectors in the economy
2. Classify factor incomes — wages, salaries, rent, interest, profit
3. Add undistributed profits, mixed income of self-employed
4. Exclude **transfer payments** (pensions, gifts — no productive service rendered)
5. Add **Net Factor Income from Abroad** (NFIA)

**Data Source:** Income tax returns, employer reports, books of accounts

**Used in India for:** Trade, Transport, Professional services, Public administration

**Precaution:** Only **factor incomes** are included. Transfer payments (pension, unemployment allowance) are **excluded** as they don't represent current production.

---

#### B) EXPENDITURE METHOD (Outlay Method)

**Concept:** This method estimates National Income by adding **all final expenditures** incurred in the economy during the year — on both consumer goods and capital goods.

**Formula:**
> **GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)**

**Components:**
1. **C — Private Final Consumption Expenditure:** Household spending on food, clothing, durables, services
2. **I — Gross Domestic Private Investment Expenditure:** Business spending on machinery, buildings, inventory (replacement investment + new investment)
3. **G — Government Final Consumption & Investment Expenditure:** Government spending on defence, education, health, infrastructure
4. **(X − M) — Net Exports:** Exports minus Imports (Net Foreign Investment)

**Steps:**
1. Estimate total private consumption expenditure (C)
2. Estimate total investment expenditure (I)
3. Estimate total government expenditure (G)
4. Add Net Exports (X − M) and Net Receipts from Abroad (R − P)

**Precaution:** Only **final expenditure** is included. Expenditure on **intermediate goods** is excluded to avoid double counting.

---

#### National Income Identity
All three methods — Output, Income, and Expenditure — give the **same result**:
> **Value of Output = Sum of Factor Incomes = Sum of Final Expenditures**

This is because every rupee spent by a buyer is received as income by the seller, and every unit of income originates from production of output.

#### Conclusion
The Income Method and Expenditure Method are two important approaches to measuring National Income. India uses a **combination of Output and Income methods** due to the diverse nature of its economy.

### **Q.6.3)** *"Explain the various reasons for the growth of Public Expenditure."* (Shortcode: **SIR DG**)

**Predicted Model Answer:**

#### Introduction
Public expenditure refers to the expenditure incurred by the **government** (Central, State, and Local) for the protection and welfare of citizens and for the development of the economy. In modern times, public expenditure shows a **continuously increasing** trend worldwide.

#### Reasons for Growth of Public Expenditure

**1. S — Spread of Democracy:**
- The majority of countries in the world are democratic
- Democratic form of government is **expensive** due to regular elections and related activities
- Conduct of elections requires huge spending on machinery, security, and administration
- This results in a continuous increase in government expenditure

**2. I — Increase in Activities of Government:**
- Modern governments are **welfare states** — they perform many more functions than before
- New functions are constantly undertaken: education, health, public works, social welfare schemes, subsidies
- Existing functions are performed more efficiently on a larger scale
- This naturally increases expenditure

**3. R — Rapid Increase in Population:**
- India's population was 121.02 crores (Census 2011) and continues to grow
- Greater population requires greater expenditure on food, housing, education, healthcare, infrastructure
- Per capita expenditure must be maintained or increased for growing numbers

**4. D — Defence Expenditure:**
- Defence expenditure is increasing even during peacetime due to hostile international relationships and security threats
- Modern warfare requires sophisticated and expensive equipment (missiles, aircraft, submarines, cyber security)
- Large armed forces, veterans' benefits, and strategic preparedness require massive spending

**5. G — Growing Urbanization:**
- Urbanization is a global phenomenon — rural populations migrate to cities
- Greater expenditure is needed on urban infrastructure: water supply, roads, energy, transport, sanitation, housing, waste management
- Expansion of urban areas requires continuous capital spending

**6. Inflation:**
- The general price level rises over time
- Government buys goods and services at ever-increasing prices
- Even maintaining the same level of services requires more spending due to inflation
- Salaries, pensions, and dearness allowances also increase with inflation

**7. Industrial Development:**
- Government implements various schemes and policies for industrial growth
- Setting up public sector enterprises, providing subsidies, developing industrial infrastructure (SEZs, industrial corridors)
- All contribute to increasing public expenditure

**8. Disaster Management:**
- Natural disasters like earthquakes, floods, cyclones, droughts, and pandemics require huge emergency spending
- Relief, rehabilitation, reconstruction, and compensation to affected populations
- Social unrest and epidemics also necessitate increased government spending

#### Conclusion
In the modern era, government functions are expanding in scope and scale. Every country, whether developed or developing, is a **welfare state** striving to provide better living standards for its citizens. This leads to a continuous and inevitable increase in public expenditure. The trend is likely to persist as governments take on more responsibilities in education, healthcare, infrastructure, and social security.
